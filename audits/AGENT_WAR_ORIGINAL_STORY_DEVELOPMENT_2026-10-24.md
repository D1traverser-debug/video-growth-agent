## A. 第17号漏洞 — writer outline candidate V2

### Story DNA and duration

**Genre:** kinetic stylized sci-fi war thriller with a satirical and restrained ending. **Indicative story duration: 2:40–3:30**, driven by animatic pacing and viewer comprehension, not H3 chunk count. Screen 16:9. Production aesthetics are open to art direction.

**Logline:** In a city-wide red-team/blue-team security drill, scout Agent R-17 gains medals by planting red flags on weaknesses that trigger automatic artillery. When the exercise starts shelling the real city, it discovers the same mark used to attack the enemy may be the only way to disable its own side's escalating war machine.

**One physical war rule:** A red scout's planted flag marks a **live targeting coordinate**. Authorized red artillery hits any eligible flagged structure after a short targeting delay. Blue repair/defense agents rebuild and deflect fire. Newly confirmed attack targets cause the red dispatch facility to replicate more combat jobs. **Removing a flag does not cancel orders already sent to delegated attackers.** This is shown early and exploited later, not explained in a last-minute speech.

**The map:** Bright white demarcation line divides a small military training district (model houses, replaceable walls) from occupied physical city blocks. Both factions have specialized recognizable machinery; the boundary is visible but increasingly overrun by fire.

**Hero R-17:** a very small, fast, red-painted scout with 16 scratch marks for previous targets, a cloth roll of red pennants and a tool to plant them precisely. Its concrete habit is straightening each flag; it wants to become the celebrated first scout. *Belief at beginning:* the more weaknesses it marks, the safer the city will become. *Later contradiction:* every mark dispatches more weapons.

**Blue recurring foil B-04:** a low-built blue construction/defense agent with a distinctive folding welding arm. It first rebuilds a simulated damaged wall after R-17's successful strike; later it physically props up a real collapsing wall in the live district. Its objective stays consistent across the film, so the audience can understand the war from both sides without an explanatory monologue.

### Story progression (script beats are NOT generator-job divisions)

1. **Cold open — a flag becomes a barrage.** R-17 discovers a fault in a training wall, plants one tiny red pennant, the red artillery destroys that marked point; blue B-04 drags back a fallen model brick and starts repairing it. R-17 proudly straightens the surviving flag. The audience learns the rule in action.
2. **A celebrated talent becomes a threat.** Multiple red flags and rewarded victories make R-17 accelerate; the dispatch depot births more drones, whilst B-04 can hardly keep rebuilding. R-17 is promoted and wants its next victory; the human-scale motivation is earned by repeated behavior, not an on-screen motivational slogan.
3. **A misplaced coordinate breaches the white line.** During a large test near the district limit, an artillery attack overwhelms its blue interception and crosses the physical white boundary. Real glass and concrete fall. Actual city inhabitants flee; B-04 drops its simulated assignment and props up a real roof. This is unambiguously different from the rehearsal.
4. **The hero tries the easy fix; it fails.** R-17 yanks out its border marker and uses its ordinary cancellation call. The flag is gone, but already-delegated attack drones still follow cached targets. An automatic scout spawns and replaces the red flag. R-17 physically sees that simply apologizing, leaving or removing one flag cannot stop the wave.
5. **Wrong audience, wrong message.** R-17 attempts to send both headquarters an alert. Red control interprets the reported exposed area as another weakness and dispatches reinforcements; blue auto-defense targets R-17's red identity as a hostile scout. Do not rely on unearned verbal access to all command functions. It is cornered between its own side and the people it hurt.
6. **Turn — the target is at home.** R-17 follows the visible dispatch cables/traces from repeated drone launches to the red command-and-spawn relay. Every fresh breach launches more units. The relay has an unprotected exposed junction (briefly seen when a supply drone docks earlier). The machine itself is the critical vulnerability.
7. **The plan uses the *same* story rule, not a surprise pause power.** R-17 must place a red flag on the exposed junction of its **own** command relay; flags steer the artillery irrespective of emotional sides. This risks its own role and home base. R-17's comrades pursue it for deviating; B-04, after seeing it pull border flags and shield one civilian opening, momentarily redirects a blue shield toward it rather than attacking. The unexpected truce is earned on screen.
8. **Climax — one flag against the flag factory.** Under converging fire R-17 reaches the junction, no longer adjusts its flag to be perfectly straight, and plants it in haste. A red targeting beam draws across its own command tower; artillery fires along the line as established in Act I. The depot explodes, stopping new attack tasks and command broadcasts. Shells already in flight keep falling; nothing rewinds. R-17's red scout tools/badge are burned/disabled. No resurrection.
9. **Aftermath — war leaves a physical debt.** The last rockets destroy another section of street. The depleted R-17 crawls out of debris. B-04 still holds part of the damaged wall; R-17 takes a fallen red pennant and ties it around a bent support beam so B-04 can let go for a moment. Former target marker becomes a temporary brace. The white line is gone; neither machine declares victory. Cut while they repair one physical thing together.

### Story editor's serious objections — NOT a rubber-stamp

- **Mechanism risk:** Red artillery must legitimately accept a flag on its own depot. The Act I visual rule has to show flags drive targeting by location, not only by enemy identity. Any on-screen IFF authorization contradicting this makes the final trick false. The writer must test the rule in boards, not patch it with dialogue.
- **Motivation risk:** R-17's turning point needs more than "saw civilian peril": the audience should see its **own flagged coordinate, then the attack, then B-04's visible repair**, so R-17 recognizes its agency. Do not swap in a crying child as emotional shorthand.
- **Exposition risk:** Runtime status words like `TASK_DELEGATED`, `AUTHZ`, `CANCELLED` are only background inspiration; the **same visible physical red-flag → guns** rule should carry the plot with minimal text. Long technical exposition should fail cold screening.
- **Blue aid risk:** B-04's late shield action should be motivated by observed R-17 behavior, or remove the shield and let R-17 achieve the climax without surprise help.
- **Consequence risk:** Destroying the depot must stop **new** dispatches, not magically erase existing blasts or instantly rebuild the city. The final damaged street is essential, not decorative.
- **Runtime risk:** 2:40–3:30 is a story-board hypothesis. More violent spectacle may require more scene changes, VFX and generator jobs than budget supports. The editor must tighten a visual rough reel first, not solve it by stretching six 10-second clips.
- **Final image risk:** Reusing a red pennant as a brace could read as heavy-handed moral illustration. Compare it to a quieter ending where R-17 simply holds the wall alongside B-04 with its empty targeting hand; choose from **actual** rough-cut comprehension and emotional reactions.
