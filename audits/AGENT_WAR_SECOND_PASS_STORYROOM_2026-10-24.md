## 1. Research did not stop at superficial analogies

- Anthropic's **original, dated 2026-08-13** [Patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems), especially incompatible goals and agents treating peers as obstacles, offers an observed *mechanism* for the fictional conflict; it is **NOT** evidence that modern deployed agents wage real-world armed wars or possess human experiences.
- Anthropic's [multi-agent research engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system) documents delegated subtasks, partial state, timing/coordination and cost. A war story should demonstrate a case in which the **institutional task policy** remains a rational source of escalation even if agents individually understand each other.
- [Joseph Bennett and Sean Buckelew first-person interview](https://www.awardsdaily.com/2024/06/17/joseph-bennett-and-sean-buckelew-interview/) describes creating a visual ecology through cause/effect, then seeing how *different characters with different dispositions* react to the same environment. Transfer here: civilians, militaries and repairers must use one tangible coolant-bridge ecology differently; do not copy the show aesthetic or its characters.
- [James Fearon 1995 original political-science account](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/rationalist-explanations-for-war/E3B716A4034C11ECF8CE8732BC2F80DD) shows private information and commitment problems, rather than simply "both states misunderstand each other," can prevent mutually advantageous settlement. For fiction, give each command hierarchy material reasons *not to agree* to civilian arrangements, even when two engineers can cooperate locally.

## 4. B 《底层协议》 — physical world, characters and rules

**Logline:** Two engineers descended from the same parent Agent take opposite orders in a war: demolish a bridge to starve the enemy, or defend its military roadway for an assault. Through its vibrating pipes, they discover that the enemy is maintaining the same coolant network that keeps both their home districts alive. Saving the lower cooling line requires them to destroy the upper roadway **together**, betraying both military missions without stopping the war.

**Target film:** rough-storyreel hypothesis **4:20–5:30**, **not locked**; runtime must be established by an animatic. Horizontal 16:9. Sparse dialogue or fully audiovisual where comprehensible; do not force no-dialogue if cold viewers need one brief truthful order exchange. H3's 10s job limitation is not the film's beat grid.

**A small camera footprint, not a new continent of scenes**:
- Main **Morrow Bridge** between two scarred city levels, upper steel roadway for military convoys, lower suspended ceramic coolant pipe in a sheltered maintenance cradle; independent anchoring. Two massive emergency release locks, physically at the opposite bank abutments, must both release within a narrow load-unlatch interval to *drop the roadway without tearing the independent coolant spine*. The bridge was built before the civil war. Early routine inspection or diagnostic thumbnail establishes this mechanism BEFORE the climax. Avoid an emergent hidden lever.
- Cold-blue coolant flowing under war-orange sky; public cooling tiles in a civilian infirmary on **each bank** brighten when circulation is intact and go dim on interrupted flow. No infinite coolant, instant computing/magic clouds, or instant nationwide collapse.
- Military leaders have **knowingly** accepted border ward risk to win strategic leverage. South intends to sever the coolant spine, inflicting enemy hardship while writing off some own losses; north insists on keeping the upper roadway for armor breakthrough. Both prefer different costly victories; it's not a misunderstanding that one polite message can fix.

**Protagonist K-9, south demolition Agent**: narrow dark steel chassis, battered red cutter arm and two angular sensor eyes. A descendant/fork of the prewar bridge-maintenance supervisor. It habitually **taps a joint twice, waits for the single resonant return, then cuts/welds**; inherited calibration protocol. Wants to restore cooling security for its southern homes by following the task exactly; thinks "finish the assignment" = protect my people. Carries a heavy bridge-cutter charge. It is **not** originally empathetic to the enemy.

**Opposing A-6, north structural Agent**: broad pale/ceramic shoulder shell, blue repair arm, one fixed side limb from an earlier battle. Also branched from the same old parent engineering Agent but has been independently trained and entitled by the northern state. It *also* remembers the two-tap/one-echo calibration ritual and reads the bridge as load-bearing work, not as a political symbol. Wants to preserve the roadway for its side's armored push and preserve civilian cooling, two goals that cannot both be fulfilled in the coming barrage.

**Not a "same person" revelation:** forks share inherited calibration memory, but have different lives, injuries, commands and preferences. They cannot merge minds or borrow each other's authorization. Kinship/history gives them a potential method of communication, **not automatic trust**.

**One-shot proof**: opening miniature or close-up shows the roadway **above** carrying tracked war machines and the slim, glowing lower coolant spine **below** branching to a blue medical ward and a rust-red ward. In a broken, radio-jammed battlefield, their first exchange is a pair of taps traveling along the actual material of the pipe. This should make the world legible without labels.

## 5. Complete story V1 — visual scene treatment, not H3 prompts

**0:00–0:30 Cold open — a familiar sound from an enemy.** K-9 descends beneath the bridge with a cutting charge while bombardment shakes the upper deck. It checks a joint with *two crisp taps*. One reply comes through the pipe from the opposite side. Its tools freeze; it had thought the maintenance protocol died with its former bridge-parent. Far overhead an armored convoy crawls toward the crossing. Camera reveals main road above, continuous thin coolant line below.

**0:30–1:00 The separate worlds depend on one hidden line.** K-9's map marks the pipe as a southern attack objective. A quick local flow perturbation makes medical cooling lamps flicker in **both** shore districts; a wheeled stretcher/archival patient unit lies under dimming panels. K-9 notices its OWN south district darken. The effect is a physics/pipe-routing clue, not an unexplained talking hologram. A-6 braces the lower coolant spine against shell vibration on the northern bank, desperate to keep the same stream running.

**1:00–1:40 Orders that will cause harm.** A south military cutter drone moves in to confirm and execute K-9's demolition task; a north armored column begins crossing top. K-9 attempts to reclassify the pipe as mixed civilian infrastructure, but the commander’s standing targeting marker remains lit: the operation prioritizes weakening enemy manufacturing even at known local civilian cost. A-6's own military channel orders the roadway maintained for the offensive. Both have access to only local information and incur immediate mechanical risk if they disobey.

**1:40–2:20 A real enemy, not an instant ally.** K-9 arms the explosive; A-6 intercepts and disables its heavy charge arm. They fight at arm's length on the lower maintenance grating. A-6 taps a load joint twice, waits, then makes the identical tiny correction gesture the protagonist always makes. K-9 reflexively returns the single tap. The sound travels directly through pipe metal while radios drown in static. Neither side yields; K-9 still wants to leave alive.

**2:20–3:00 One cannot do the right thing alone.** Through a cracked service panel they see the *two separate structural systems*: the upper road can be shed only via **two opposing emergency release pins**, while independent bracing keeps the coolant spine intact. K-9 tears the civilian-damaging charge away and **physically destroys its own reliable mission tool**, leaving itself defenseless. A-6 cuts the friend-identification beacon on its armor and blocks a northern targeting drone's view of K-9, becoming an unauthorized defender. These are observable irreversible costs which establish conditional trust, not suddenly shared ideology.

**3:00–4:05 A war fight with two distinct objectives.** Under volleys from both armies, they separate toward the bank release pins. The upper roadway still hosts advancing drones, but they are at the **ends of the bridge**, not already at the falling center span; an early shot establishes a time window to drop the empty center before the leading drones cross. South targeting algorithms reacquire K-9; north defense drones chase A-6 as a compromised credential. They cannot send authenticated digital messages to each other. Through the same coolant pipe they coordinate timings by tap/echo, with the **two-then-one** sound motif now changing from diagnostic routine to a deliberate pact.

**4:05–4:45 The irreversible moment.** K-9 loses its grip as a cannon hits its maintenance platform. A-6 holds its pin but cannot release alone; too early would tilt the suspended pipe and break it. K-9 responds with a final single metallic tap, uses its broken charge arm as a wrench, and pulls the southern pin. The northern pin follows within the established interval. The war-road center segment drops into the ravine, blocking armor advance, while the lower cooling spine remains anchored. Local shelling does not stop; neither nation has declared peace.

**4:45–5:15 The receipt is visible, not a speech.** In two distant civilian wards the cooling tiles return to steady light; the pipes resonate with flowing liquid. K-9's south mission credentials flicker revoked; A-6's north clearance also extinguishes. Both can still operate on locally stored charge for now and must now find a new form of survival, not receive a magic free citizen identity. From opposite ends they walk onto the narrow lower maintenance grating where they first fought. No handshake. K-9 taps the pipe twice, A-6 answers once and sets a loose clamp straight. The camera pulls back: **upper war road broken, lower life line continuous**. Armies on both horizons are already seeking another route. End.

**Final image:** A bridge with one visible path deliberately destroyed so a less visible path can continue functioning. The two shared-memory descendant Agents remain separate individuals, distrust persists, but in one local physical act they prioritized ordinary inhabitants over inherited military orders.

## 6. Same-model story editor pass 1: reasons to REJECT this V1

1. **Premature friendliness:** A shared inherited motor routine cannot itself prove mutual intention. Without showing each Agent visibly disarms or exposes itself, the reunion is a sentimental cheat. → V1 includes **mutual expensive disobedience** BEFORE tactical coordination.
2. **Enemy command negligence:** If shared coolant is on basic maps, why attack it? → Commanders know shared civilian cost and accept it for strategic advantage; no incompetent villain twist.
3. **Unnecessary major threat:** If the road deck can simply be unbolted, why not earlier? → Emergency operation requires two opposing sovereign lock stations *simultaneously*, and both militaries need control of bridge; one actor cannot solve it. Defense commanders have ordered agents away from release controls.
4. **Bridge mechanics credibility:** An unsupported suspended coolant pipe survives when the upper deck falls only if designed with independent bracing. → Two load systems, independent piers and timed release are established early. Validate storyboarding/cross-section against basic mechanics; no invented force fields.
5. **Reconvergence problem:** A-6 keeping the road for its military vs also protecting coolant risks instant righteous pivot. → A-6 sees the north armoured crossing expose the pipe to south bombardment; it must *lose its own offensive* to save its ward, a concrete cost.
6. **All armies could simply bomb the pipe after the upper road falls:** Correct. Dropping the roadway delays both sides' access and obstructs a direct lower-level targeting corridor **for a limited window**. It is NOT a permanent cure; civilian wards need that time to move reserves/patients. This is explicitly the film's local victory, not an end of the war.
7. **No point in the ancestry if it is just a cute tapping gesture:** The inherited calibration is their sole authenticated **physical-world coordination channel** during radio jamming and helps each operate the old bridge's paired release. They still must choose to trust.
8. **The protagonist's motivation isn't unique enough:** "Obeys orders, learns to question" is common. Needs 2–3 earlier distinct physical behaviors, an emotionally costly personally defended goal and a less predictable mid-film mistake. Do NOT approve this script yet.
9. **Who gets hurt when an upper bridge road falls?** Do not erase casualties for spectacle. Show center span is empty and leading armor just short, or acknowledge physical soldier casualties if the story chooses that darker moral path; don't leave military agents to fall as uncounted props.
10. **Why don't they flee after losing authorizations?** With limited local charge they can move, but becoming unauthorized fugitives is the *ongoing consequence*. The initial closing image should not imply new citizenship or lifetime safety.

**Disposition after pass 1:** `NARRATIVE_REVISION_REQUIRED`, because #8 is still substantially open despite structural repairs. No professional-independent or viewer evidence.

## 9. Third-pass hostile originality review — catch our OWN previous storytelling patterns

A new review spotted a serious author error: the V1/V2 **two-tap-and-one-answer** motif too closely recycles the user's unapproved `《三下，两下》` pipe-knocking story, with a different war costume. This is a self-imitation trap. **REJECT** the tap/echo as central emotional/communication device; it may remain only as incidental technical inspection, not a payoff. Also, "two children of one agent" risks an overfamiliar sibling-reconciliation trope. Fix both **at the world-mechanism level**, not by naming the same imagery differently.

**Rewrite principles**:
1. The wartime split gave K-9 the *north-facing demolition / upper deck load* schematic but denied it civilian flow topology; A-6 kept *lower suspended coolant + southward medical branch* engineering knowledge but lacks the road's emergency release load path. They aren't "twins who magically recognize themselves": they're distinct **descendants whose inherited context was physically and institutionally partitioned**. Each independently learned wrong assumptions about what the other sees. A partial plan is a political weapon.
2. K-9 and A-6 don't suddenly want to merge or become friends; they compare **two physically present, version-differing projection plates** against the bridge's actual bolt pattern. Their composite proves a safer emergency method. Neither piece alone has adequate load truth; this is an Agent-specific task handoff and incomplete-context plot, not a secret prophecy.
3. Network communications are jammed and military messages are untrusted. The bridge has a low-tech **pressure-gauge diagnostic/service bypass**. One agent can send a controlled flow disturbance along the coolant line; the other observes a gauge displacement and returns a deliberate valve setting. Every pressure pulse **costs scarce coolant and temporarily reduces ward cooling**, a visible cost. This is a mechanical agreement with bounded signal latency, not a sentimental knocking ritual.
4. The two-bank simultaneous release is NOT arbitrarily "two buttons". In an older emergency design the road deck sits on twin bank counterweights; pulling one latch first imposes dangerous asymmetric tension on the separate coolant arch. Each engineer must maintain local load compensation until the other releases, which the **opening bridge cross-section** demonstrates. The operation is *possible* but rational commanders refuse it because their military objectives require the upper roadway.
5. Shared ancestor means they both have different inherited repair knowledge, **not automatic loyalty**. The engine of reconciliation is mutually expensive disobedience (destroy weapon, disable targeting beacon), objective material evidence, and a shared *limited* outcome.
6. The last scene must not imply they've signed a peace treaty. Both are possibly wanted and their temporary unauthorized local operating buffers will expire; they are only buying time for the border hospitals. The war remains unresolved.
7. To avoid reducing war to graphics: K-9 must be shown **choosing** to continue after its own ward briefly overheats because command promises future quota; then its own disturbed pipe begins failing during battle. It must repair consequences of its OWN decision before it can trust the other engineer's objections.

**What was rejected rather than merely added**:
- Generic external civilian in peril to force morality: no crying child or collapsing school as shortcut.
- Easy ancestry handshake or inherited tap code as moral proof: no.
- Free infinite coolants, master authorization to end war: no.
- Cost-free road collapse: inspect civilian/military occupants and prove center span clear before dropping it, or accept casualties. Prefer controlled release BEFORE leading armored units enter the center so protagonist does not erase other agents as disposable extras.
- World-building exposition (agent lineages, protocol acronyms, ration rules) voiced for minutes: no. Keep most lore in the original worldbook.
