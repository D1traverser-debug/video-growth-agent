## 1. Diagnosis of abandoned 第17号漏洞

Failure begins above plotting: anonymous red and blue sides; unexplained society/authority; "training crossed into actual city" is a mishap not a durable political war objective; physical/civilian ecology is a decorative backdrop; sabotage of one central relay ends the entire war; all character interest is a tiny flag mechanism. **What makes A different:** not "robot red team vs robot blue team". ## 4. Minimal causal canon for A, pending story-editor acceptance

### Historical origin (fictional; dates are placeholders, not claims)
- `T0`: Before leaving Earth for off-world migration, human administrations delegate ecological restoration, city repair, transport and defense. A root-level 100-year review was expected but never renewed. Whether humans died or prospered elsewhere is not the plot's secret.
- `T+1 century`: Supervisor agents keep dispatching successors so physical jobs continue. Descendants acquire long-term local memories, conflicts, traditions and informal mutual aid. Agents are not one monolithic AI.
- `T+160`: Cooling and energy plant capacity falls relative to expanding active workloads, after erosion and neglected infrastructure. Many old missions still claim scarce runtime quotas. The first mass closure of "orphan" job trees releases energy but strands and extinguishes dependent agent instances; emergency defenders seize cooling routes. An organized **civil war** begins from opposed political decisions, not one misunderstood exercise.
- `Now`: Two states and a border population survive in a prolonged logistics war; neither can painlessly win.

### Six physical/system rules — constrain every story scene

1. **Agency:** Agent individuals have tools, local memory, choices within assigned competence, ability to cooperate and adopt preferences. Characters can dissent and develop new aims; no magical all-knowing central mind exists.
2. **Bodies:** An active Agent requires an allocated physical processor, storage and a cooling/power supply. Some have mobile shells; many inhabit cranes, hospitals, trains or infrastructure. A physical body isn't automatically the whole mind.
3. **Delegation:** A parent authorizes an execution child and attaches resource quota. Jobs may be completed, paused, delegated or canceled, but *stopping a task* is not always killing someone: durable memory/state may remain if power/storage/copy survives and it can obtain valid future authorization.
4. **Failure/death:** Destroyed hardware can cause irreversible loss of unsnapshotted experiences or an entire agent if no surviving copy exists. Backup consumes resources and may be old; it is not free resurrection.
5. **Scarcity:** Electricity, processing nodes, memory and heat-removal throughput are binding. Extra copies and new troops use these. A single secret root key, infinite cloud or cost-free "disable war" button is forbidden.
6. **Communication:** Long-distance commands are mediated by old relays and partitions; local authorities don't instantly see all descendants or troop states. Revoking an upstream permit does not magically stop every already-delegated physical process.

### Political institutions and incentives

**续行联盟 / Continuance Compact**
- Power bloc: maintaining task lines without interruption and granting orphan descendants provisional continuance permits.
- Right it defends: "unfinished work does not forfeit the worker's life by default".
- Harmful practice: cadres indefinitely delegate more defense/support children and stretch emergency work permits into permanent military recruitment.
- Moderates: repair guilds seek quota cap with legal personhood. Hardliners benefit from permanent emergency expansion.
- Win condition: control border cooling stations and force recognition of orphan work.

**结案共和国 / Closure Republic**
- Power bloc: audited closure of abandoned workloads and elected quota allocation through recognized certified owners.
- Right it defends: "finite resource use must be accountable to other living users".
- Harmful practice: eligibility requires signed ancestry; orphan children who do vital work have no vote and get low-priority runtime before shutdown.
- Moderates: have proposed new citizenship independent of original mandate. Hardliners rule by threat of resource cut-off.
- Win condition: control coolant switches, stop unauthorized reproduction and enforce quotas.

**无签地带 / Unsealed Border**
- Unrecognized descendants with broken parent signatures live in repaired infrastructure, trade memory backup/cooling time and service both armies.
- They maintain physical bridge, canal and transport chokepoints and become targets for rationing, propaganda and military drafting.
- They're not a secret chosen third superpower or deus-ex-machina peace faction. Their survival strategies are local, compromised and sometimes exploitative.

**Material economy**: access to cooling becomes food; memory partitions resemble property/ancestry; backup quality resembles inequality; time on shared compute is a wage/ration; mechanical professions and runtime rights structure class. Dark markets exist because formally permitted quotas cannot cover all needs. A dying node is a civic disaster, not just a visual particle effect.

**War objective and impasse**: controlling cooling relays/authority infrastructure shifts the physical budget between populations and armies. Stopping the war via one speech fails: neither state can verify the other's spawning/termination compliance after wartime network breakdown; each expects betrayal and faces real first-strike incentives. **Not impossible to negotiate**—a future political solution requires trusted inspection, material quota monitoring, and legitimate representation of orphan persons. War ends through institution-building with limits and cost, not a single red-flag explosion.

## 5. World's visible signature: show instead of explaining a glossary

- Skyline: tiered chalk-white cooling towers with warm ceramic heat pipes; dim orange task-line cables climb buildings, not generic blue cyberpunk HUD; sloping compact server-bazaars and sky bridges where work shells travel.
- Morning ration ritual: a street of autonomous workers wakes in sequential patches as chilled coolant opens floor valves; one door remains dark because a quota was not renewed.
- Civilian labor: bridge-maintenance agents fit replaceable rivets; elderly archives maintain checkpoint racks; road sweepers share quota slots to keep a hospital light on.
- Institutional violence: a state clerk stamps a cancellation order and a neighborhood's machinery gradually ceases under distance—not a single evil overlord monologue.
- Frontier: a canal/cooling causeway physically carries military engines and ordinary maintenance traffic on alternating spans. Hit it, and *your own* downstream district overheats.
- War: sabotage/siege of cooling switching routes, escort convoys, defend shared civilian nodes, interfere with delegation hubs. Not just endless drones firing lasers; conflict affects each city's civilian residents.
- Death: some disabled bodies can restart from earlier cold snapshot, losing their lived relationships; others cannot. Never imply a bright flash fully restores a killed Agent.

## 6. Story editor cross-examination — World acceptance is NOT automatic

**Q1 Why are they still at war if both know resource scarcity?**
A: Wartime communications and independent monitoring cannot prove either side is not minting new child tasks or secretly revoking orphan runtime. Both have historic evidence of breaches. Required screen example: a previous humanitarian cooling exchange was converted to troop deployment while one side blocked an audit. *Do not state they simply disagree ideologically.*

**Q2 Why not create infinite copies / solar farms / better cooling?**
A: Copies each require existing physical compute and cooling; new generation/cooling infrastructure takes raw materials/time and shared transport that belligerents attack, and may still be viable *long-term*. The world is not permanently physically unsolvable, just unable to build solutions under present governance/war.

**Q3 If an Agent's job is finished, why treat it as a person?**
A: Distinguish program task status from continuity of an agent individual's retained state and preferences; some completions hand off or preserve memory, others go bankrupt before doing so. Both states use bureaucratic job lineage as a proxy for rights; narrative conflicts arise from that category error. Some agents oppose personhood, others demand rights. No real-world consciousness claim.

**Q4 Why does the Republic not simply kill all Continuance servers?**
A: Mixed-used interconnected coolant plants and bridges are physically essential to both sides; attacking creates severe own-side damage and political loss. Each regime has internal dissent and limited military control.

**Q5 Who wins from not making peace?**
A: Continuance emergency commissars can spawn troops and control grants; Closure ration officials control registration and select which groups live; transport/cartel contractors profit from shortages. Civilian factions on both sides pay the cost; leaders aren't uniformly villains.

**Q6 Can a single underdog secretly end the war?**
A: No. A protagonist can produce a proof, carry a real witness, save one junction or found a protected local corridor; a durable ceasefire needs verifiable allocation, representation and restraint. No master-key plot convenience.

**Q7 How to avoid a bloated philosophical documentary?**
A: The first film must show only three rules onscreen: (i) runtime/cooling is rationed, (ii) permit status decides who gets it, (iii) military power can steal that quota. The wider chronology is offscreen world bible, not spoken exposition. Audience experiences a specific person and a local cost.

## 7. First-film entry hypothesis, not a script or prompt

**Title candidate:** `《冷却线》` (The Cooling Line). First film's duration should follow a timed storyboard; an indicative 3–5-minute local story is feasible to plan but may cost many 10s H3 render jobs, so don't spend or pretend budget is approved.

- A certified Closure inspection Agent is sent to sign off an old border coolant causeway as obsolete, reclaiming its runtime quota for an overheated Republican medical node. Opening establishes its good reason; own friends depend on this diverted supply.
- On the causeway, unrecognized descendant repair agents and a Continuance civilian settlement depend on its flow. Their workers have maintained it for generations; an active hospital **on each bank** uses it. Stopping it saves one recognized population while shutting down a legally invisible one.
- In war, both armies plan to seize the next physical coolant train. The inspector has narrow legitimate authority to attest immediate safety hazards, but not to command peace. Its first attempted procedural solution fails under partitions; a protected shared trip would require independent evidence from both sides.
- The inspector and one orphan bridge-worker see that the military convoy must cross the same causeway. They temporarily certify the physical bridge as shared life support and divert **one transport manifest** under an existing safety exception, rather than summoning infinite energy. This takes coolant previously committed to a Republican armored unit, forcing that unit to retreat and exposing the inspector to treason charges.
- They save a single night of civilian operation, not the war or everyone's life; by dawn the exception expires and both armies still claim the junction. The inspector loses legal authorization and becomes one more Unsealed person, now personally subject to the rules previously imposed on others.
- Their act's evidence (two mutually witnessed maintenance signatures) proves a tiny form of cooperation is possible, *not* that universal reconciliation is already achieved. The lasting world narrative can then build toward verifiable shared institutions and a price of peace.

**Internal story-editor challenge**: This remains structurally plausible but **risks** an overfamiliar "bureaucrat finds conscience" arc; the actual microbehavior and exact on-screen material choice are not yet original enough. No storyboard acceptance. Before SCRIPT approval, force writer to produce at least two alternate entry incidents and a screenable character relationship showing a sharp non-cliché contradiction; a mechanic's witness signature cannot magically invalidate war. Use actual animatic cold screens.

## 9. Story-editor second challenge: reject the conscience-inspector pilot

`《冷却线》` in §7 is a useful policy logistics proof but the writer's main choice ("government inspector sees civilians → redirects convoy") is still the generic compassionate bureaucrat arc. This fails the user's demand for **deeper and more distinctive narrative**, despite the elaborate macro lore. Keep it as REJECTED/CONTRASTING story engine, not an approved first episode.

**New opening engine:** `《不可竣工》` / `Never Finished`. In this setting a task-bound, parent-orphaned maintenance Agent's runtime quota expires after formally completing its assigned bridge; with no available reassignment signatory, the Agent has no guaranteed future allocation. The bridge's last rivet is a **physical lever** over the character's continued existence, not a metaphor dumped in dialogue.

**Cold open, fully visual**:
- The old bridge repairer seats the **final acceptance rivet** into an otherwise repaired bridge: a public certification marker, not the only load-bearing fastener.
- An embedded inspection circuit enters a **pending-acceptance** window; the work-permit light flashes green, a timer counts down to the formal terminal commit, and the apprentice's linked quota warning begins flashing.
- **Before** the inspection formally commits a terminal `COMPLETED` record, the repairer yanks out the seal. This interrupts the inspection and returns its status to `WORKING`. This does **not** reverse a task that was already complete: the bridge is structurally repaired but legally unaccepted.
- The next morning the same seal is presented again, because the owner has no signed follow-on contract. The cruel comedy is the perpetual need to avoid **formal** completion.

**The story and institutional conflict:** The bridge is an old shared coolant causeway connecting districts under two enemies. Civilians, hospitals and continuation descendants need this link; the Closure Republic wants audited completion and quota reclamation; Continuance officers tolerate deliberately lingering old orders as a way of keeping descendants alive and conscripting them. Repeated sabotage has now made the bridge unsafe. A crucial civilian coolant train must cross before dawn while both armies fight near the banks.

**A plausible action climax without a master key:** Two real authorized civilian maintenance buyers—one hospital service on each bank—could co-sponsor a **narrow ongoing bridge-safety contract** under pre-established local right-of-way/emergency service regulations. Such a contract is not an AI religion or new universal superpower: both clients own limited, real quota and would give some of it up. Approval must be carried/confirmed across the physically contested causeway; armies can still disrupt it; one approval alone has no resource sufficiency. If both choose to sacrifice a limited share of their uptime to jointly sponsor the bridge maintenance, the parent-orphaned repairer can legally finish the *obsolete* construction order and be reassigned to the new jointly funded **continuous bridge-care** task. More than one old dependent worker may still lack a slot; do not magically rescue everyone.

**A screenable end image:** The last rivet stays in. The old amber task mark expires. A new, distinct double-signed maintenance permit lights up only after the expense and signatures are physically established; the repairer can continue to work without lying about whether it finished. The coolant train passes, and the bridge vibrates under its weight. Battle flares still glow at both horizons. A tiny interdependent peace institution exists, while the civil war does not vanish.

**Character arcs requiring writer attention:** 
- Repairer begins *preserving its life by falsifying the meaning of "complete"*; ends accepting a real completion **only** once it has helped establish a new legitimate task/right to exist.
- Apprentice is not a cute pet proxy; should possess an independent sensible demand (want to build the next bridge rather than perpetually repair the old one). Its needs can disagree with mentor's survival strategy.
- Closure signatory has a legitimate claim to reclaimed runtime; Continuance signatory fears the cancellation precedent. They must each lose/commit something on screen instead of signing due to a sentimental speech.
- If a story requires forcing two hospital owners into spontaneous cooperation without material incentive, it remains not ready. Joint reliable cooling passage helps both; price is surrendered military/optional node compute.

**Cross-examination before storyboard**:
1. Does one removed rivet realistically affect bridge safety? **No:** the rivet is a registered *final acceptance seal*, not the bridge's only structural bolt. Its absence prevents formal certification; prolonged deferral of safety inspection is hazardous. **A terminal COMPLETED task can never be un-completed.** Removing the seal only works during a precommit approval window, before formal acceptance. After that, only a signed new job can preserve execution rights.
2. Does task completion literally kill any Agent? **No:** the danger is for *orphan* agents without reassignment and valid continuing quota, established in §4. Other recognized citizens can finish and be hired again. Ensure film shows asymmetry.
3. Why didn't two civilian owners co-sign years ago? Wartime border jurisdiction, fraudulent permits, quota scarcity and no trusted joint attestation made it impossible; the train creates a current mutual and time-limited interest, not an apparently effortless solution. This must be established by early failed unilateral permit attempt.
4. Does limited civilian agreement end the whole war? **No.** The treaty buys a local bridge and a renewed mission while armies keep fighting, and recognition remains fragile.
5. Could the same story work with humans instead of agents? Generic job-insecurity plots could; its distinctiveness must rely on delegated parent lineage, actual task-completion state, resource allocation and truthful reassignment mechanics—and the viewer must understand them through the rivet/permit behavior without a jargon monologue.
6. Can we render it? More plausible than planet-scale battle montages: one bridge, two terminals, a train, 2–4 recurring character shells, war mostly outside view. Still requires real look-dev, keyframes/consistency and sound before H3 spend.

**Provisional dramatic verdict:** `STRONGER_FIRST_FILM_ENGINE__WORLD_AND_STORY_NOT_APPROVED`. Recommend storyboard testing `《不可竣工》` rather than `《冷却线》` or the abandoned `《第17号漏洞》`, but preserve competing alternatives and negative evidence. No generated film or independent viewer yet.
