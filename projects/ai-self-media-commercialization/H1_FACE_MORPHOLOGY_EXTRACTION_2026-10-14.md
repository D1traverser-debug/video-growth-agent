## Reliability boundary

This is a frontal certificate-style image and is useful for 2D proportion and primary mass relationships. It is not reliable for exact side-profile projection, cranial depth or unretouched skin texture.

Do not infer as truth:
- skin smoothness / brightness;
- makeup finish;
- retouched symmetry;
- exact nose projection from profile;
- exact jaw depth from side;
- hairstyle or suit styling.

Use all measurements/ratios below as qualitative-to-semiquantitative design guidance, not biometric identity replication.

## Neutral structural read

### 1. Overall face envelope
- Overall read: moderately elongated oval / soft-rectangular face, not a narrow V-face.
- Maximum visible width sits around upper cheek / zygomatic region rather than at the jaw.
- Temples do not collapse inward strongly.
- Lower face tapers gradually from cheek to jaw; the taper is controlled, not sharp.
- Chin is compact and rounded-blunt rather than pointed.
- Jaw corners are soft but present; they should not be erased into an idol-style taper.

Design value:
- preserve cheek support + compact chin relationship;
- avoid increasing lower-face taper;
- avoid lengthening the chin.

### 2. Vertical proportion
- The face does not read as bottom-heavy.
- Midface length is moderate.
- Lower third is neither unusually short nor unusually long.
- Forehead/hairline is partly obscured by hairstyle, so upper-third measurement is unreliable.

Design value:
- do not exaggerate long midface;
- do not extend the lower third to create a generic “handsome” face.

### 3. Brow / orbital system
- Brows are relatively straight with low-to-moderate arch.
- Brow mass sits fairly close to the eyes; there is no exaggerated high-brow beauty spacing.
- Eye sockets read horizontally and calmly rather than sharply angled.
- The orbital system is a better same-source anchor than copying the exact eyelid shape.

Design value:
- preserve relatively horizontal brow-eye organization;
- preserve moderate brow-eye compression;
- avoid high arched brows or deep heroic brow ridge.

### 4. Eyes
- Eyes are horizontally oriented and moderately narrow in vertical aperture.
- The outer corners do not show a strong aggressive upward cant.
- Inner corners are visible but not heavily stylized.
- Inter-eye spacing appears balanced; do not compress eyes toward the nose or spread them dramatically.
- The portrait shows small left-right differences, but a single retouched frontal photo is not sufficient to canonize exact asymmetry.

Design value:
- horizontal calmness is worth inheriting;
- moderate narrowness can be increased slightly for the IP, but should not become fox-eye / villain-eye styling;
- do not use micro-asymmetry as the primary identity carrier.

### 5. Nose
- Nose bridge reads moderate rather than extremely high or razor-narrow.
- Nose width is natural relative to the eye spacing.
- Tip is compact / softly rounded rather than needle-sharp.
- Alar/nasal base is not unusually narrow.

Design value:
- preserve natural nose volume and width;
- reject hyper-perfect narrow CG nose;
- future 3D design needs a separate side-profile decision because this image cannot define projection reliably.

### 6. Mouth / philtrum / lower-face relation
- Mouth width is moderate and does not dominate the lower face.
- Lip contour is restrained; upper-lip shape is not extremely sharp.
- Neutral mouth line is close to horizontal with a mild natural softness.
- Mouth-to-chin spacing supports a compact lower face rather than a long pointed chin.

Design value:
- keep mouth moderate and low-drama;
- preserve compact mouth-to-chin relationship;
- expression identity should come from baseline tension/relaxation, not from artificially shrinking the mouth.

### 7. Cheek / jaw / chin masses
- Cheek region has more structural value than V1 captured.
- Face does not naturally collapse directly from cheek to a pointed chin.
- Jawline transitions are soft, but there is still lateral support in the lower face.
- Chin center is compact and not long.

Design value:
This is one of the strongest same-source features to preserve.
The IP should likely strengthen—not weaken—the cheek/jaw support to escape generic CG-male tapering.

### 8. Neck / head transition visible in portrait
- Neck appears relatively long and slim but not extremely thin.
- Head-to-neck transition is clean.
- The certificate pose is formal and straight, so it does not define the final natural posture.

Design value:
- plausible source for a lightly elongated neck;
- do not use the suit collar to infer neck width.

## What is worth inheriting

### Strong same-source anchors
1. moderately elongated soft-rectangular/oval face envelope;
2. cheek support stronger than a V-face template;
3. compact, blunt/rounded chin rather than long pointed chin;
4. horizontal brow-eye organization;
5. moderate-narrow eye aperture without aggressive tilt;
6. natural medium nose volume rather than narrow CG nose;

### Optional anchors to compare, not automatically lock

### Explicitly do not inherit

- skin smoothing;

- current black hairstyle;

- “handsome optimization” from the source image.

## Face-design hypothesis space for H1

The source should generate multiple character architectures rather than one edited likeness.

### F1 — Same-Source Natural
- closest to the real structural envelope;
- preserve cheek support, compact chin, horizontal eyes;
- only mild stylization;
- strength: strongest creator resonance;
- risk: can fall back into “virtualized creator.”

### F2 — Blunt Analytical
- slightly broaden cheek and jaw masses;
- shorten/strengthen chin mass;
- keep eyes calm and horizontal;
- reduce beauty taper;
- strength: strongest anti-generic identity and adult stability;
- risk: can become too heavy/ordinary if overdone.

### F3 — Editorial Plane
- keep same envelope but clarify brow plane, cheek plane and jaw plane;
- slightly leaner soft tissue while preserving mass support;
- strength: strong photographic/fashion presence without idolization;
- risk: can drift into runway-model severity.

### F4 — Compact Deadpan
- slightly reduce perceived lower-face length;
- keep cheek width and compact chin;
- slightly reduce eye aperture and expression amplitude;
- strength: strongest deadpan/readability at thumbnail scale;
- risk: can become too stylized or “sleepy.”

### F5 — Controlled Uncanny Human
- preserve human anatomy and source envelope;
- introduce one subtle proportion anomaly only (for example slightly unusual brow-eye compression OR cheek-to-chin transition), not multiple distortions;
- strength: strongest IP distinctiveness;
- risk: can become synthetic/alien and weaken commercial trust.

## Independent recommendation before visual comparison
