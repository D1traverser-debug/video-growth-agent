## Research synthesis translated into character-design rules

1. Do not maximize masculinity as a proxy for attractiveness. Reviews and recent facial-attractiveness research show that symmetry/normality/averageness can matter, while greater male sexual dimorphism does not reliably improve attractiveness. For this IP, avoid heroic brow, oversized square jaw, high narrow nose, and other generic male-model shortcuts.

2. Preserve East Asian facial plausibility. Asian eyelid literature repeatedly emphasizes natural, ethnicity-consistent eyelid architecture rather than Westernized deep-set/high-crease solutions. For the digital sculpt, use a low/subtle upper-lid crease logic, keep orbit depth conservative, and avoid deep-set fashion-model eyes.

3. Male brow shape is a high-leverage variable. A 2025 East Asian eyebrow morphology/preference study found triangular-straight eyebrows most preferred for men. Translation for this character: straighter brow body, low-to-moderate arch, clear but not dramatic lateral apex, denser/cleaner brow body, and a tapered resolved tail.

4. Eye design should emphasize horizontal refinement rather than enlargement. Use modest lateral lengthening, stable vertical aperture, cleaner upper-lid contour, firmer lower lid, and near-level to minimally positive canthal tilt. Avoid large round eyes and fox-eye exaggeration.

5. Do not overuse symmetry. Naturalness/normality matters; perfect mirrored geometry can look artificial. Preserve mild organic asymmetry later, but not at the gray-sculpt comparison stage unless it is deliberate and stable.

6. Front-view nose/chin changes should be conservative until 3/4 validates depth. Front view cannot reliably establish projection. Do not attempt major nasal projection, chin projection or jaw-angle redesign before a stable 3/4 sculpt exists.
