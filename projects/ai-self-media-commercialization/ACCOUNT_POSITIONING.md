# Account Positioning — V1

## Core identity

**Category:** AI-native cinematic technology-business media.  
**Public promise:** explain complex technology, business/capital and internet-culture systems with an engineer's analytical lens and high-impact AI visual storytelling.  
**Do not position as:** AI-tools account, stock-picking account, generic finance account, car-review account, crypto-alpha account, game streamer, beauty account, or generic AIGC spectacle account.

## Core thesis

> Technology changes money; money changes technology; the internet amplifies incentives, errors and human absurdity.

The account's recurring editorial question is:

> **What are the rules of this system, what incentives drive it, where are the bugs, and why do people behave this way?**

## Creator-fit inputs

Use as editorial advantages, not as literal public bio copy:
- IC-DV / semiconductor verification engineering background;
- financial research / A-share review-system experience;
- prior Solana memecoin / meme-culture exposure;
- some competitive-game experience and familiarity with game-system incentives;
- AI fiction / storytelling experimentation;
- interest in agentic production systems and automation.

## Audience

Primary audience hypothesis:
- roughly 20–40;
- interested in AI, chips, digital hardware, smart vehicles, technology companies, business models, markets, internet culture and emerging technology;
- values explanation, evidence, mechanisms and visual clarity more than daily news recitation.

The channel should attract a **commercially coherent audience**, not maximize unrelated views.

## Content pillars

### 1. Technology / AI / Chips / Smart Hardware — ~40%
Examples:
- why AI training consumes so much compute;
- what a chip bug actually means;
- how autonomous-driving compute perceives the road;
- why AI PCs / edge inference / new hardware matter or do not matter.

### 2. Technology Companies / Business / Capital — ~35%
Examples:
- where AI datacenter money goes;
- why technical leadership does not guarantee commercial victory;
- why a company may burn capital for years;
- how technology, supply chain, margins and valuation interact.

### 3. Internet Culture / Meme / Digital Society — ~25%
Examples:
- why a meme can become a billion-dollar asset;
- what memecoins actually trade: utility, identity, attention or coordination;
- how FOMO and community narratives form;
- why apparently rational digital systems create absurd outcomes.

## Smart-vehicle role

Smart vehicles are not a separate identity. They sit at the intersection of:
- chips;
- AI;
- software;
- engineering systems;
- consumer hardware;
- technology-company strategy.

This provides a natural bridge into automotive brand budgets without turning the account into a traditional test-drive channel.

## Primary recurring formats

1. **工程师拆世界** — mechanism/system explanation.
2. **科技公司的账本** — technology × business/capital.
3. **互联网疯人院** — meme, incentives, attention, collective behavior.
4. **未来已经来了** — smart vehicles, robotics, AI devices, next-generation consumer hardware.

Names are working titles and may change; their editorial functions are more important than the labels.

## Main content form

Primary test format:
- 6–10 minute Bilibili-oriented cinematic AI explainer / mini-documentary;
- later 12–20 minute flagship pieces if retention and production economics justify it.

Derivative distribution:
- 45–120 second re-authored short versions for Douyin / Video Accounts / Xiaohongshu;
- not simple clip dumping: short-form must rebuild hook, pacing and conclusion;
- later English rewrite + voice + localized graphics for YouTube where topics travel internationally.

## Monetization hierarchy

Platform playback income is **supplemental**, not the model foundation.

Priority revenue stack:
1. native brand sponsorship / custom integration;
2. content production and publishing fees;
3. paid-media / material-usage / licensing rights as separate commercial items;
4. recurring series sponsorship / season partnership;
5. product or commerce conversion where editorial fit is genuine;
6. memberships / platform monetization / audience support as additive income.

## Target advertiser pools

Primary commercial adjacency:
- 3C / digital hardware;
- AI software / productivity tools / network services / cloud;
- smart vehicles / vehicle technology;
- smart home / appliances / smart hardware;
- games;
- e-commerce platforms and major technology campaigns.

Do not distort editorial identity merely to chase categories with high ad spend. Product truth and audience trust override category opportunism.

## Sponsor integration rule

Content must work before sponsorship is added.

Preferred structure:

`worthwhile question -> evidence / mechanism -> story -> sponsor product genuinely belongs -> integration -> return to story`

Avoid generic mid-roll ad reads where a product can instead be used as a real case, tool, system or verified example.

## Financial-content boundary

Public content may cover:
- industries;
- companies;
- financial statements;
- business models;
- market history;
- valuation concepts;
- incentives and capital behavior;
- investment education.

Do not build the public commercial model around paid stock recommendations, guaranteed returns, private stock groups or individualized buy/sell instructions.

Private recommendation/review systems are separate research engines, not this account's public commercial identity.

## Crypto / meme boundary

Allowed editorial framing:
- internet culture;
- attention economics;
- coordination and community;
- market history;
- mechanism analysis;
- speculative psychology;
- digital-asset culture.

Do not make the domestic commercial model depend on exchange referrals, transaction diversion, paid alpha groups, coin calls or trading-service promotion.

## Brand character role

The original digital character is a **Digital Observer / Narrator**, not a virtual news anchor.

Expected use:
- recurring visual anchor;
- opening/closing or transition presence;
- entry point into impossible/invisible systems;
- world-building and channel recognition;
- selective thumbnails and brand materials.

It should not occupy every shot or simply sit and read narration.

## Success criteria for early pilots

Do not optimize only for views. Establish channel baselines for:
- click-through rate;
- first-30-second retention;
- average view duration;
- completion rate;
- follow conversion;
- saves / shares / comments;
- viewer-quality signals;
- production cash cost;
- creator-hours per published minute;
- brand-fit / sponsorability;
- repeatability.

The first 12 formal pilots can be balanced across the four editorial functions, but the actual pilot count and cadence should remain adjustable to production evidence rather than fixed as doctrine.
