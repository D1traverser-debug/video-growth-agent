# H0 Reference Assessment — 2026-10-14

Status: ACTIVE_H0_INPUT
Scope: HUMAN_CORE_ARCHITECTURE_V2

## Executive conclusion

The creator's body should **not** be the primary identity reference. For this long-term digital IP, recognition priority is head/face first, then head silhouette/hair/accessory, then body silhouette/posture/movement.

Body references remain important as plausibility constraints and motion/wardrobe anchors, but should not force a literal physical copy.

Operational principle:

> Face carries identity; body carries credibility, silhouette, motion and garment behavior.

## Current photo set

### R-FACE — certificate portrait
Primary role:
- facial morphology source.

Useful for:
- face length/width tendency;
- forehead-to-brow relation;
- brow-eye spacing;
- eye spacing and orbital placement;
- nose/mouth relative placement;
- cheek/jaw/chin distribution;
- adult Chinese male age/gender anchor.

Do not inherit:
- whitening;
- skin smoothing;
- makeup/grooming intensity;
- hairstyle;
- suit styling;
- photographic color cast.

Reliability:
- HIGH for frontal facial proportion relations;
- LOW for true skin value and natural grooming.

### R-LIFE — outdoor full-body life photo
Primary role:
- real-world body plausibility and casual physical presence.

Useful for:
- overall tall/slim read;
- broad head-to-body proportion tendency;
- leg length tendency;
- shoulder slope tendency;
- relaxed standing posture;
- natural adult-male physical presence;
- approximate true skin value under non-studio conditions.

Limitations:
- oversized T-shirt and loose trousers obscure torso/pelvis construction;
- pose and camera distance reduce anatomical precision;
- footwear alters visible lower-leg/foot proportion.

Reliability:
- MEDIUM for overall silhouette and limb-length tendency;
- LOW for exact torso/waist/pelvis anatomy.

### R-GRAD — graduation group photo, creator at right
Primary role:
- secondary cross-check for body height, limb length, shoulder/arm presence and standing balance.

Useful for:
- adult male height/presence relative to another person;
- long-limb tendency;
- hand/arm scale impression;
- shoulder slope and neutral standing balance;
- natural skin value cross-check.

Limitations:
- graduation gown strongly masks torso, waist, pelvis and garment-body relationship;
- shorts/gown combination breaks clean proportion reading;
- group-photo perspective introduces minor comparative distortion.

Reliability:
- MEDIUM for height/limb/posture cross-check;
- LOW for torso construction.

## Revised reference weighting policy

This is not a numerical model-weight setting; it is design-authority priority.

### Human Core identity priority
1. FACE / HEAD STRUCTURE — dominant
2. HEAD SILHOUETTE / HAIR ARCHITECTURE — secondary
3. BODY SILHOUETTE / POSTURE / MOVEMENT — supporting
4. SKIN / GROOMING — supporting realism
5. WARDROBE — downstream, not identity source

### Personal inheritance policy

Face:
- use selected structural resonance from the creator;
- do not pursue one-to-one likeness.

Body:
- use creator body as a plausibility envelope, not a target mesh;
- preserve adult Chinese male, tall/slim origin, realistic shoulder/limb logic;
- allow stylization of head-to-body ratio, neck length, shoulder/pelvis relation, leg proportion and hand/foot scale when it improves the character system;
- reject extreme runway elongation or adolescent fragility.

## Identity stress test

A successful V2 Human Core should remain recognizable when:
- rendered in grayscale;
- hair is temporarily dark;
- eyes are temporarily dark;
- pixel glasses are removed;
- clothing is replaced by a neutral fitted base layer.

If the identity collapses under those tests, branding/styling is carrying too much of the character.

## Current decision

Body is **not** a primary likeness source.

Use the two full-body photos to set:
- plausibility boundaries;
- tall/slim adult origin;
- long-limb tendency;
- shoulder/posture truth;
- skin-value cross-check;
- later movement and garment-fit constraints.

Do not use them to copy exact torso shape or to force the final digital character to match the creator's real proportions literally.
